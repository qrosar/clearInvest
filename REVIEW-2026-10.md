## 1. Critical — wrong today, and readers act on it

### 1.1 ETF strategies are simulated before fees; bank products after

**What.** Bank products use `defaultRate = grossBaseline − TER` (e.g. KBC Pricos: 5.50% − 1.53% = 3.97%). ETF strategies use `defaultRate = s.historicalReturn` ([products.ts:75](lib/calculator/products.ts#L75)), and `historicalReturn` is the **index** return — `monde-simplifie` says so itself (*"Basé sur l'indice MSCI World en EUR"*). The engine only subtracts TER for custom products ([compute.ts:141](lib/calculator/compute.ts#L141)), on the assumption that presets have it "baked in". For ETFs, it isn't.

The breakdown then tells the reader the opposite: `calculator.tax_detail_ter_note` — *"TER déjà reflété dans le taux de rendement historique utilisé."*

**The same error is on the ETF guide.** `comprendreEtf.etf_detail` says *"IMIE · ~8% brut · TER 0,17%"* → `etf_value` *"~408 000 €"*. €408k is exactly €3,600/yr at 8.00% for 30 years — no TER. At 7.83% it is **~€395k**, so the headline *"+112 000 € — frais uniquement"* is closer to **~€97k**. The hero chart and `s7_comparison_intro` reuse the €408k.

**Why it matters.** The site's credibility rests on comparing like with like. This tilts every ETF-vs-bank comparison toward the ETF by 0.07–0.30%/yr (≈2–8% of the 30-year result). The conclusions survive the fix — the credibility of the comparison does not survive a reader finding it.

**Fix.** In `ETF_PRODUCTS`, use `s.historicalReturn − weightedTer`, except for `esg-mondiale`, whose figure is already net of fees (`netOfFees: true` in [historicalReturns.ts](lib/strategies/historicalReturns.ts)). Update the golden values in [compute.test.ts](lib/calculator/compute.test.ts), then the prose figures that quote them (see 2.3). Re-derive `comprendreEtf.etf_value` / `diff_value` and `s2_p2`'s "25 à 35%".

### 1.2 The cash ETF (CSH2) is described at a 3% rate that no longer exists

**What changed.** ECB deposit rate: 2.00% from 11 June 2025, **2.25% from 17 June 2026, 2.50% from 16 September 2026**. €STR trades a few basis points below, so CSH2 currently earns ≈2.4% gross, ≈2.3% after its 0.10% TER. For most of the past year it was ≈1.9%.

**Where the site still says ~3%:**
- `strategies.ts` → `epargne-liquide.historicalReturn: 0.03` — the calculator default.
- `data.strategies.epargne-liquide.why_0` — *"Rendement actuel ~3% brut, supérieur aux meilleurs comptes épargne belges"*. The best regulated accounts pay 2.60–2.85% including the loyalty premium (vdk Rythme 2.85%, Belfius Flow 2.80% — the site's own figures, still accurate this month).
- `…epargne-liquide.tagline` — *"Battre le compte épargne, sans risque"*. On the engine's own numbers (€10k + €300/mo, 5 years) CSH2 at 2.4% ends at **€30,316**, behind vdk (€30,857) and Belfius Flow (€30,805). And "sans risque" is not a claim to make for a swap-based fund with no deposit guarantee.
- `historicalReturns.ts` → `epargne-liquide.y1: 0.029` labelled *"€STR approx 2025"*. The ECB rate path puts the 2025 €STR average near **2.2%**. **verify**
- `ethiasAnalysis.s2_table_row3_val3` — *"~2,90 % (€STR, variable)"*, and the 9-year conclusions built on it in **Ethias, AG Safe+ and Belfius Capital Safe**: *"Sur 9 ans, CSH2 fait mieux (+25 900 €…)"*. At ≈2.3% net, €20,000 grows to ≈**€24,500** over 9 years — behind Ethias (€24,900) and AG Safe+ (€24,800), still ahead of Belfius (€23,900). These pages are dated April 2026, when €STR was ≈1.93%, so the figure was already stale when written.
- `ethiasAnalysis.s2_objection_p` — *"CSH2 n'a jamais enregistré de perte sur aucune période de 12 mois depuis sa création."* **False.** €STR (and EONIA before it) was negative until September 2022; overnight-rate ETFs lost money over 12-month periods in 2019–2021. The site's own 10-year €STR figure (1.2%/yr) reflects this.

**Fix.** Make the cash copy rate-agnostic: describe the mechanism ("≈ €STR − 0.10%, today ≈ X%") instead of a level, set the calculator default from one constant, and drop "sans risque". Then re-run the three Branche 21 comparisons and rewrite their 9-year verdicts honestly. At today's rates, a guaranteed 2.75–2.90% beats CSH2 *if you hold to maturity*. CSH2's case is liquidity and no lock-in, not yield. The verdicts can still land against Branche 21 on the 30-year horizon.

### 1.3 The foreign-account guide states the wrong legal test

`declarerCompte.intro_info`: *"Ce guide s'applique à tout broker dont le siège social est hors de Belgique… La nationalité du broker… détermine l'obligation."*

MeDirect (Malta), Saxo (Denmark), Keytrade (France) and Trade Republic's new Belgian branch all have their head office abroad. Two paragraphs earlier, `intro_p1` correctly says their clients have nothing to declare. The test is whether the account is held with an establishment **in Belgium**, branches included — the same distinction the broker page and the CGT memory already use.

This is the one page whose purpose is telling readers about a legal obligation. Fix `intro_info` in all three locales: *"…tout compte ouvert auprès d'un établissement situé hors de Belgique. Une succursale belge (MeDirect, Saxo, Keytrade, Trade Republic IBAN BE) compte comme belge."*

### 1.4 The engine taxes regulated savings interest at 30%

All eight savings accounts in [products.ts](lib/calculator/products.ts) use `taxConfig: { interestTax: 0.30, interestExemption: 1020 }`. Above the €1,020 exemption, regulated-account interest is taxed at **15%**. The site says so correctly in `comprProduits.s1_fiscal_text`, `comprFiscalite.table_val_above_threshold` and `glossaire.term_precompte_def`.

The engine, `calculator.tax_note` (*"30% … pour les comptes épargne, 15% pour les comptes réglementés"* — the accounts listed *are* regulated) and every `data.products.savings-*.description` (*"exonérés du précompte de 30%"*) contradict that. The impact is small: it only bites above ≈€36k on deposit. But it shows up as a line in the tax breakdown.

**Fix.** Set `interestTax: 0.15` on the eight savings accounts (keep 0.30 on bons de caisse), and correct `tax_note`.

## 2. High — factually wrong statements

### 2.1 Pension savings funds are not "branche 23"

The 8% tax on a **fictive 4.75%/yr capital** applies to pension savings **funds**. Pension savings held through **insurance** (branche 21 or 23) are taxed on the actual reserve. The site attaches the fictive rule to branche 23:
- `comprFiscalite.tax5_applies` — *"fonds d'épargne-pension (branche 23)"*; `tax5_insight` — *"fonds de placement, branche 23"*
- `comprProduits.s4_pt2_body` — *"comme si votre fonds (branche 23)… s'applique aux fonds d'épargne-pension de branche 23 uniquement"*
- `glossaire.term_branche23_def` — *"La taxe anticipative de 8% à 60 ans est calculée sur un capital fictif…"*
- `analyseProductsPage.cat_pension_title` — *"Épargne-pension (Branche 23)"*

A reader holding a branche 23 pension-insurance contract is told the wrong tax base. Replace "branche 23" with "fonds d'épargne-pension" and add one line for insurance-based pension savings.

### 2.2 The TOB rule is stated three ways, and VWCE contradicts itself

The 1.32% rate applies to accumulating funds **registered for public offering in Belgium** (FSMA). Most of the site says so. Three places use a different test:
- `comprendreEtf.s6_bullet3` — *"0,12% pour les ETF capitalisants **domiciliés** hors Belgique"*. VWCE is Irish and pays 1.32%.
- `calculator.custom_tob_hint` — *"1,32% pour fonds **domiciliés** en Belgique"*
- `premierAchat.s4_item2_desc` / `s4_warning` — 1.32% means the ETF is *"enregistré comme OPCVM"* (every UCITS ETF is an OPCVM), *"le plus souvent une erreur de sélection (mauvaise ligne de marché)"*. TOB is set per fund, not per exchange; picking another listing of the same ISIN changes nothing. A reader deliberately buying VWCE would be told to abort a correct order.

**VWCE:** `comprFiscalite.tax1_insight`, `tout-en-un.why_0` and the glossary state 1.32% as fact. The three `alt_*_verdict` keys on the strategy pages say *"le TOB applicable varie selon le broker — de 0,12% à 1,32%"*. Pick one — the registration-based 1.32%, which is also what `strategyContent.ts` encodes.

### 2.3 The pension-vs-ETF claim quoted from the simulator is stale

`comprProduits.s4_verdict_p3`: *"un ETF Tout-en-un dépasse l'épargne-pension Argenta **dès la 11e année** — et termine avec **33 000 €** de plus."* Also `glossaire.term_epargne_pension_def` ("dès la 11e année").

The current engine (€87.50/mo, 30 years, 30% relief) gives **crossover in year 13 and +€29,390**. After the 1.1 fix: year 13, **+€25,883**. Quote the engine's output, or drop the precise year (see 7.3).

### 2.4 MSCI World holds no China

`biais-chine.description` (*"au-delà de ce qu'inclut un ETF Monde standard (~3%)"*) and `why_0` (*"un ETF monde classique n'alloue que ~3% à la Chine… soit 10× plus"*). IWDA/MSCI World is developed markets only — 0% China. The ~3% figure is MSCI ACWI. Rephrase as "0% in IWDA, ~3% in a global ETF such as IMIE".

Related: `anti-us.description` says the US is *"~65% d'un ETF Monde"*; `anti-us.why_0` and `biais-europe.why_0` say ~70%. Use one figure (≈72% today).

### 2.5 FAQ slips

- `questions.r1_p3` — *"En 2020, -35% en mars, +70% sur l'année."* +70% was the rebound from the March low; the 2020 calendar return was about +16% in USD and +6% in EUR.
- `questions.r3_p3` — *"Dans les années 2010, les marchés émergents avaient surperformé… ont ensuite subi une décennie de sous-performance."* Emerging markets outperformed in the **2000s** and lagged through the **2010s**.
- `questions.r4_p2–p3` — the Schwab study figures don't match its published results as I recall them: immediate investing finished ~11% behind perfect timing and roughly **3×** ahead of staying in cash, not "30 à 40%". **verify** against the source and cite it.
- `questions.r2_p2` — *"En 2024… environ 35 000 € à 40 000 €."* Refresh to end-2025.
- `questions.d3` — covers the €100,000 cash guarantee but not the €20,000 investor-protection scheme for securities a failed broker cannot return. Worth one sentence.

### 2.6 50% bracket quoted at the 2023 threshold

Four pension analyses (Argenta, Belfius, BNP, KBC) say *"tranche marginale de 50 % (revenus imposables > ~46 440 €/an en 2026)"*. €46,440 was the 2023-income threshold. It is €48,320 for 2024 income and **€49,840 for 2025 income**.

### 2.7 First-order guide

- `premierAchat.s2_imie_name` — *"iShares MSCI ACWI IMI"*. IMIE is **State Street SPDR**, as the strategy data says.
- `premierAchat.s5_tip` — *"Plusieurs brokers (MeDirect, Saxo) proposent… des plans d'investissement périodiques."* `brokers.ts` has MeDirect at `savingsPlan: false`. Use Trade Republic and Saxo.
- `premierAchat.s2_tip` — *"préférez la cotation en EUR… pour éviter le risque de change."* The trading currency changes conversion costs, not currency exposure: an S&P 500 ETF is USD-exposed whichever line you buy. On an educational site this misconception is worth correcting rather than repeating.
- `premierAchat.s2_csh2_name` (*"Amundi EUR Overnight Return"*) vs `strategies.ts` (*"Amundi Smart Overnight Return"*) — align with the current justETF name. XEON is listed above CSH2 in the ISIN table, although it was demoted to an alternative.

### 2.8 The legal notice contradicts the licence

`mentionsLegales.s3_p2`: *"Toute reproduction, même partielle, est interdite sans autorisation préalable."* The About page, footer, README and LICENSE all say **CC BY-NC 4.0**, which permits non-commercial reproduction with attribution. Replace with a pointer to the licence.

### 2.9 Wallonia mortgage dates

`comprProduits.s_lt_mortgage_p1` puts Wallonia's cut-off *"avant 2016"*, `p2` *"après 2025 en Wallonie"*, `p3` *"après 2016 (Flandre/Wallonie)"*. The regional *chèque habitat* covers Walloon loans from 2016 to 2024; loans from 2025 get no regional benefit at all. Either way, the three paragraphs should agree.

## 7. Improvements

### 7.1 Homepage
- The three featured strategies are Simple (IMIE), **"Performance" (Nasdaq-100)** and ESG. Featuring a 100-stock tech bet under the label "Performance", with *"Rendement historique élevé"*, contradicts the strategies page's own advice (*IMIE for "la grande majorité"*) and the Nasdaq page's warning. ### 7.2 Compliance tone
The site positions itself as education, not advice, but a few passages read as instructions:
- `strategiesPage.recommendation_body` — *"Achetez l'ETF IMIE… celle que la grande majorité… devraient suivre"*

Conditional phrasing ("pour un investisseur qui…, la solution la plus simple est…") keeps the substance. Absolute historical claims should be scoped ("depuis 1970, en EUR") and sourced.

### 7.3 One source of truth for numbers quoted in prose
The pension crossover year has drifted twice, and fees differ across pages (3.1). Two cheap guards:
- a test that runs the engine scenarios quoted in copy and asserts the strings (crossover year, 30-year gap);
- a test that parses `TER x,xx %` out of each analysis namespace and compares it with `products.ts`.

### 7.4 Reconcile the analyses' model with the engine
Open since August: the analyses use annual compounding with no ETF TER and no buy-side TOB; the calculator uses monthly compounding. After 1.1, both at least agree on fees. Standardising fully on the engine would let the analyses quote exactly what a reader can reproduce in the calculator.

### 7.5 Cite sources and publish a methodology note
- `calculator.bank_data_note` says the standardised 8%/3% baseline is *"conforme aux recommandations FSMA pour les projections standardisées"*. Cite the FSMA document or remove the claim.
- SPIVA (85–92%), Schwab (timing), Vanguard (lump sum ~2/3) and the MSCI factsheets are each referenced without a link.
- A short methodology page (assumptions, sources, update cadence, what "brut/net" means on each page) would answer most "where does this number come from?" questions in one place.

### 7.6 Content worth adding
- **"Déclarer ses plus-values en 2027"** — first CGT return: reclaiming the exemption, losses, the 31 Dec 2025 reference value, and foreign brokers.
- **"Les taux remontent — que faire de son épargne ?"** — savings accounts vs bons de caisse vs CSH2 vs Branche 21 at today's rates, which would replace scattered claims with one maintained comparison.
- The five "analyses en cours de rédaction" (KBC Sivek, Carmignac Patrimoine, ING Easy Invest, Belfius Flex Invest, KBC plan) have been promised since April. Publish them, or remove the section; the `cat_investment_plans_*` copy written for them is unused.
